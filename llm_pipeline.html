Tokenization & Subword Splitting
Breaking text into the model's vocabulary units
Text is split into subword pieces (using an algorithm like BPE or WordPiece). A word like "unhappiness" becomes un + happi + ##ness — three known pieces. This lets the model handle rare and new words without needing them in the dictionary. Tokens starting with ## are continuations (they attach to the previous piece).

Vocabulary & Vocabulary Size
The fixed dictionary all tokens come from
The model has a fixed vocabulary — every possible token and its integer ID. GPT-style models typically use ~50,000 tokens. Larger vocabularies mean shorter sequences but a bigger embedding table; smaller vocabularies need longer sequences but less memory.

5
The [UNK] Token
What happens when a word isn't in the vocabulary?
With word-level tokenization, any word not in the dictionary becomes [UNK] — the model loses all information about it. With subword tokenization, rare words are split into known pieces, so the model keeps some understanding even for unseen words.

⚠️ Word-level tokenizer

"unbelievable" → [UNK]
"happiness" → [UNK]
"transforming" → [UNK]
All information about these words is lost. The model only sees [UNK].

✅ Subword tokenizer (BPE)

"unbelievable" → un believ ##able
"happiness" → happi ##ness
"transforming" → transform ##ing
All pieces are in the vocabulary. No information is lost.

Embeddings (Vectors)
Token IDs become dense numerical vectors
The model contains an embedding table: a giant matrix where each row is a learned vector of floating-point numbers for one token. Looking up a token ID retrieves its row — a vector of typically 512 – 4096 numbers. Below, 8 dimensions are shown for illustration. 🔵 Blue cells = negative values, 🔴 Red cells = positive values.

Spelling-Based vs Meaning-Based Representation
Why embeddings capture semantics, not just characters
Simple character matching thinks "cat" and "cap" are similar (they share "ca") and "cat" and "kitten" are different. Embeddings learned from billions of words know the truth: cat ≈ kitten (both small felines) and cat ≠ cap (completely different categories). Similarity here is cosine similarity (0% = opposite, 100% = identical).

🔤 Spelling-Based Similarity (character overlap)

Based on longest-common-subsequence over characters. High = many shared letters.

💡 Meaning-Based Similarity (embedding cosine)

Based on where words cluster in embedding space. High = similar meaning.

3D Embedding Visualization

Embeddings live in hundreds of dimensions — impossible to visualize directly. PCA compresses them to 3 dimensions while preserving as much structure as possible. Nearby points = similar meaning. Notice how animals, vehicles, and fruits each form tight clusters, far from one another.
